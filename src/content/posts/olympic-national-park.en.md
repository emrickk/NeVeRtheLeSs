Our first day in Olympic National Park went to the fog. Hurricane Ridge is on the north side of the park; in clear weather, apparently you can see Canada. We couldn’t even see the mountains on our own side. The road bent into white, each row of trees fainter than the last, until only a few nearby branches remained. We had driven up for a distant view and ended up with plenty to look at close by. We had never seen fog quite this thick.

The next morning we had breakfast at Lake Crescent. Forest covered the slopes right down to the water, the lake tucked between them. Breakfast was sandwiches. Nothing elaborate planned, but choosing this spot to eat was already a pretty good arrangement.

We drove around the peninsula on this trip, and the Hoh Rain Forest was the stop that called for an early start. We arrived around eight, with cars already queuing at the entrance. Everyone knows to come early to a national park in summer. Fortunately the queue didn’t follow us into the forest. They held cars at the entrance, leaving room for people and traffic to spread out inside.

This is what I like about national parks in summer: having a little room. A road that gets you in, then a proper walk without being pressed into a crowd the whole way. The forest was big, the trail just a narrow strip through it, and even the fallen trees had things growing on them. We saw plenty of squirrels along the way.

Back in the car, we carried on around the peninsula. Beyond this stretch of forest, the road still had trees on both sides.
