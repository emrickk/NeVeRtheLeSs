On our first morning in Amsterdam the sky was gray. The hotel window came in two pages: on the left, a row of old houses packed shoulder to shoulder; on the right, a clock tower in the distance. No two houses were the same height, and each wore a different hat. Together they looked like a family portrait.

![The hotel room's double window over Amsterdam's rooftops](https://cdn.theneverless.com/2026/07/amsterdam-hotel-window-room.webp)

In the afternoon we slipped into the Nine Streets, a little net of lanes woven between the canals, shops standing one against the next. We turned into a café to rest our feet, small and busy. The door stood open, and over it ran a band of stained glass, orange and blue squares set in a white grid. It took coming to the Netherlands for it to click: Mondrian may have learned his grids from doorways like this one.

![A couple leaning on the railing at a bridge in the Nine Streets](https://cdn.theneverless.com/2026/07/amsterdam-nine-streets-couple.webp)

![Looking out the open door of the café, with stained glass above it](https://cdn.theneverless.com/2026/07/amsterdam-cheese-shop-door.webp)

In the evening we boarded a canal boat. Amsterdam really is best toured from the water. The boat turned into the old town, and a tower leaned out from between the window frame and its reflections. It is called the Montelbaanstoren. Four hundred years ago, when the clock on top was new, it rang even at midnight, and the people of Amsterdam nicknamed it Malle Jaap, Crazy Jaap. It did not make a sound as we passed. Probably tired.

![The glass-roofed cabin of the canal boat, with the EYE museum across the water](https://cdn.theneverless.com/2026/07/amsterdam-glass-boat-eye.webp)

![The Montelbaanstoren between the window frame and reflections](https://cdn.theneverless.com/2026/07/amsterdam-montelbaanstoren.webp)

All through Amsterdam I kept turning one thing over: why are the windows here so big? Houses have big windows, boats have big windows, the café gave its whole street-facing wall to glass, nothing like American houses with their small, careful windows. And yet the Dutch are famous for their boundaries: they say what they mean and keep the rules in plain sight. Oh well. Maybe that's just the Netherlands.
